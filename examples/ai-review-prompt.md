# Literature review prompt

Copy everything below the line into your AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, …) and
attach the three files it names. Replace `<TOPIC>` and `<OUTPUT DIR>`.

---

You are helping me review the literature on **<TOPIC>**.

Attached files (generated by `paper-puller`):

- `candidates.json`: the search results. Each record has `title`, `year`,
  `venue`, `doi`, `abstract` (may be empty), `export_key`, and `matches`
  (which queries found it and at what rank).
- `selected.bib`: the same records as BibTeX, keyed by `export_key`.
- `corpus_summary.json`: what the local corpus covers (venues, years, abstract
  coverage), so you know what could *not* have been found.

Please produce `<OUTPUT DIR>/review.md` with these sections:

1. **Scope**: one paragraph on what the queries cover and what the corpus
   leaves out (venues, years, missing abstracts).
2. **Themes**: group the relevant papers into 3–8 themes. For each theme give
   2–4 sentences, then the papers as `[export_key] Title (year), DOI`.
3. **Key papers**: the 5–10 most relevant papers, one line each on why.
4. **Excluded**: candidates that matched the queries but are off-topic, with a
   one-line reason each.
5. **Gaps and next queries**: open questions, plus 3–6 new search queries in
   SQLite FTS5 syntax (e.g. `"task allocation" AND (energy OR battery)`),
   formatted as a JSON array I can save to `examples/<topic>-queries.json`.
6. **Read in full**: papers whose relevance can't be judged from the metadata.

Rules:

- Cite only papers in `candidates.json`, using their `export_key` and DOI.
  Do not invent papers, findings, or DOIs.
- Mark claims based only on a title (empty `abstract`) as **(title only)**.
- Search rank is lexical match strength, not paper quality; don't present it
  as quality.
